# Board: DevSetu

**Vote: 2 pass, 1 fund if. Average score: 4.3** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 6).

No member voted to fund the idea as pitched. Offers and Monopoly passed. Product would fund only if the catalog is cut to one door and the public site shows the working prices. A pass here is the result of this meeting.

Offers stays a pass if the only reason a buyer says yes is a price near the cost of serving them. Monopoly stays a pass until a small market, a way to reach it, and one lasting advantage are all in the brief together.

## Risks more than one member raised

**The buyer is several people, so the market is unpicked.** All three. Individuals are early developers, people switching into this work, and people who are aspirational about these systems. No income, no geography, and no split of who pays. Companies are a second buyer: an enterprise package for onboarding new college graduates, with no price and no described session. Offers will not score pain, money, reach, or growth on that list, and will not fund an offer until one buyer is named. Monopoly says a durable business takes a large share of a small market first; this plan opens wide, across engineering disciplines, at a mass price. Product says three products and two buyers means nothing is the one thing done brilliantly.

**The price is a wide-audience price, and the cost behind it is unmeasured.** All three. A track is ₹250 to ₹500 a month, a minor is ₹500 a year, and Cinder is ₹750 a year. The leaning is the cost of serving one learner plus about 30%. That cost is not measured, and whether 30% sticks is not decided. Offers: if buyers show up only because the price sits near that cost, the offer is a commodity and the pass stands. Monopoly: a wide opening at a guessed margin is a fight for thin margins, and shared machines are not an economy of scale until the cost is shown to fall as volume grows. Product: the cheapness rule sits on the live machine, which is the product. The first five minutes work only if that shared machine is there, is a real environment, and grades at once.

**Nobody has said how the first customers arrive.** Offers and Monopoly. The brief states that the README does not say how customers find the product. Monopoly treats that as fatal on its own: distribution is half the business, and a public app is a product, not a path to a share of any market. What each of those customers costs is unstated. Offers scores the same blank as a market that cannot be reached.

**Nothing in the brief makes the outcome believable, or hard to copy.** Offers and Monopoly, from different sides. There is no guarantee, and no stated proof that a learner gets a job or passes an interview. Instant grading shows an exercise was checked. Offers scores likelihood as the place offers die: the buyer still does the builds, Paper of the Month is one attempt with no price, and the Whiteboard is off. Monopoly will not treat the subjects as a moat. They are landmark systems and papers named in the brief. Community access has no size, so it is not a network effect. A brand, on these facts, is a name and a public site. What is known here that others do not know is unstated.

## Where they disagree

Product scored a 6 and voted fund if. Offers scored a 4 and passed. Monopoly scored a 3 and passed. Product is looking at a moment that already exists: read a short lesson, do the work on a live machine, fail without taking a system down, get graded at once, and, on a project, finish a working system. Offers and Monopoly are looking at an unpicked market, an empty path to customers, and no lasting advantage. Those are empty on the facts in the brief. Cutting the catalog, which is what Product asks, does not fill them.

They want different things cut. Product wants one door: the systems lab the projects already name, or one track that is that lab. Paper of the Month stays out of what a customer can buy until it has a price and a finished exercise. Tracks across every discipline wait until that one path is the thing done brilliantly. Offers does not pick the door. It picks the buyer, one of the early developer, the switcher, the aspirational learner, or the company, and then asks for a guarantee and a price that buyer accepts because of the working system. Monopoly leaves the small market open. It will not treat the systems projects as that market until a share of some smaller set is the plan, and until who already sells to that buyer is known. Those three instructions are not the same first move.

They want different things from the price. Product will fund the loop once the site shows ₹250 to ₹500 a track a month, ₹500 a minor a year, and ₹750 for Cinder a year, and once cost to serve is measured. Offers will pass if that price is the only difference a buyer can see. Monopoly funds the shared lab only if the measured cost is an advantage a copycat cannot match soon. A lab that is merely inexpensive satisfies none of them.

Product alone treats the public site as a broken first screen: the working prices are the offer, and amounts still shown in the app are not. The other two do not score that seam. They would still pass after it was fixed.

## The strongest version the board can see

One named buyer pays for one path: a short lesson, then the work on a live machine in the browser, with a safe failure, instant grading, and a finished system at the end of a project. The site shows the working prices. Papers and the other disciplines are not on that door. The company package, if it exists, is that same lab with a price, sold to one company buyer rather than to developers in general. Price is what that buyer will pay for the outcome, once cost to serve is measured, and there is a stated way the first customers arrive. The idea file pitches a wider catalog, tracks across disciplines plus projects plus papers, at a mass price, with an unpriced company package beside it. This version is a smaller business than that pitch. Product would fund the smaller one if the cuts are made. Offers and Monopoly would still pass it until the buyer, the proof, the path to customers, and one lasting advantage are in hand. Without those, the narrower product is a live app, and it is not yet a business either.
