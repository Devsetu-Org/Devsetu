# Competitors: DevSetu

Checked 28 October 2026. Every price and quote below is from a public page linked here. Where a page did not show a number, the cell says so. Nothing here is a testimonial for DevSetu.

DevSetu, from `founder/idea.md`, sells two paid things: a track at ₹250 to ₹500 a month (a short lesson, then graded work on a live machine in the browser), and a project at ₹500 a year for a minor or ₹750 a year for Cinder (a staged build of an LSM tree, a B+ tree, Raft, a small SQL engine, or a key-value store). Paper of the Month has no price. The company package for onboarding new graduates has no price.

The comparable item used for the price range is one month of hands-on practice. Annual prices are divided by 12 only when the seller states the annual total.

## Positioning map

Two axes that match the job in the idea file: whether the buyer does the work, and whether the work is operating a tool or building the system that holds data.

**Do the work, on a tool someone else built.** KodeKloud, Killercoda, iximiuz Labs, SadServers. The machine is in the browser. The subject is Linux, Docker, Kubernetes, or a broken server. Price runs from free (Killercoda scenarios, iximiuz and SadServers free tiers) to about $6 to $15 a month where a number is published.

**Do the work, and build the system.** CodeCrafters, for Redis, SQLite, Kafka, and Git, graded by a test run, in the buyer's own editor. Mini-LSM, MIT 6.5840, PingCAP Talent Plan, and the CMU projects, for an LSM tree, Raft, a key/value store, a buffer pool, and a B+ tree. Those four are free and run on the learner's machine, or, for MIT, as a university subject. CodeCrafters is the only paid one, and its current price is not on the public page.

**Read it, for an interview or a job.** ByteByteGo sells written system-design courses. Scaler sells a year of classes, system design inside a placement programme, at a few lakh rupees. Boot.dev sits between this and the first group: you do type the code, and the destination is a backend job, not a storage engine.

DevSetu's pitch sits in the second group (build the LSM, the B+ tree, Raft, the SQL engine, the key-value store) and also in the first (tracks across engineering disciplines, on a shared machine). The map already has occupants in both groups.

## The gap

The storage and consensus builds DevSetu wants to sell are already free, and the paid browser labs already exist for a different subject.

A learner who wants to build the system can do it today without paying. [MIT 6.5840's Spring 2026 schedule](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/6.5840/schedule.html) assigns MapReduce, GFS, and Raft, and labs for MapReduce, a key/value server, Raft, and a sharded key/value store. [Mini-LSM](https://skyzh.github.io/mini-lsm/) is a free LSM course with tests, on your own machine, and it stops before SQL and Raft. [PingCAP Talent Plan](https://github.com/pingcap/talent-plan) is a free Raft and TinyKV course adapted from MIT. [CMU's Fall 2024 project page](https://15445.courses.cs.cmu.edu/fall2024/project3/) describes a buffer pool and a B+ tree as earlier projects. The gap is not "nobody teaches this."

A learner who wants a live machine in the browser, graded, can pay $6 to $33 a month, or pay nothing on Killercoda, and get Linux, Docker, and Kubernetes. [KodeKloud](https://kodekloud.com/), [iximiuz](https://labs.iximiuz.com/pricing?showPlans=1), [SadServers](https://sadservers.com/), and [Killercoda](https://killercoda.com/) already sell that shape. The complaints say the lab sometimes does not stay up. They do not say the lab is missing.

What this pass did not find is a paid product that puts the LSM, the B+ tree, Raft, a small SQL engine, and a key/value store on a machine in the browser, graded, at a rupee price. [CodeCrafters](https://codecrafters.io/challenges) is the nearest paid product: SQLite there includes B-trees, and Kafka is a distributed system, and the catalog read today does not list Raft or an LSM tree. They grade in the learner's editor, not in a hosted lab, and in May 2026 they [stopped building new challenges](https://forum.codecrafters.io/t/were-pausing-new-challenges/16129).

That hole is real and narrow. The free courses already cover the curriculum for anyone who will install the toolchain and grade themselves. A lower price does not, by itself, occupy the hole. The board's condition still stands: the reason to pay has to be the live graded machine, and the cost of that machine is still unmeasured.

The company package is a different market, and it is already occupied. KodeKloud sells a business plan. SadServers sells training and hiring assessments from $245 a month. Scaler sells placement-oriented onboarding of early engineers at a few lakh rupees. DevSetu's enterprise price is unset, so there is no offer there yet to compare.

## The threat

The fastest copy of a **track** is Killercoda. Anyone can publish a browser scenario there, and the free tier already lets a learner use those scenarios as much as they want. KodeKloud is the fastest copy with a brand and a sales motion: the lab fleet, the cert catalogue, and a team plan are already live. A new Linux or Kubernetes track on DevSetu is a feature on their roadmap, not a new category.

The fastest copy of a **project** is a wrapper around work that is already public. Mini-LSM, the MIT Raft labs, and the CMU B+ tree project are on the open web. CodeCrafters already has the grading loop and a SQLite challenge, and its founders have stopped adding challenges, so a new Raft or LSM challenge is not what they said they would ship. A new owner of that product, or anyone who hosts those free labs in a browser, can put the same builds behind a login.

iximiuz is the closest machine. The [Tinkerer page](https://labs.iximiuz.com/pricing/tinkerer) describes real Linux VMs, Firecracker, root access, and "break things safely," at $6 a month billed yearly. That is the delivery DevSetu describes, pointed at Linux and containers today.
