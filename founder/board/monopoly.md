# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong

The platform is live and public at https://devsetu.io. A learner reads, then does graded work on a live machine in the browser, and can fail without taking a production system down. Every track uses the same shape: a short lesson, a real environment, instant grading, and community access.

Timing is stated. Language models made writing code the easy part, so the scarce skill is understanding systems that hold data, traffic, and state, learned by doing the work. The projects match that claim: an LSM tree, a B+ tree storage engine, Raft, a small SQL engine, and a key-value store, Cinder. Paper of the Month uses a named pool: the Google File System, MapReduce, Dynamo, Raft, and Bigtable.

Shared lab machines are a cost choice. A student works on a real machine without their own cloud account, so the price can stay cheap enough for a wide audience. Individual prices are set: ₹250 to ₹500 per track per month, by the infrastructure that track needs; ₹500 per minor per year; ₹750 per year for Cinder. The leaning is the cost of serving one learner plus roughly a 30% margin.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No path to the buyer.** The brief states that the README does not say how customers find the product, and that there is no stated way the first customers arrive. On this lens, distribution is half the business. A product with no way to reach buyers is not a business. What one customer costs to reach is open. `/founder-cfo` is the skill for that number. The channel is unstated, and this memo does not name one.

2. **No small market, and no share of one.** The buyers named are early developers, people switching into this work, people aspirational about these systems, and companies onboarding new college graduates. Tracks run across engineering disciplines. The price is a wide-audience price, near the cost to serve one learner, plus a margin of about 30% that is not decided. No share of any market is stated. Who else sells this within reach of the same buyer is unknown. `/founder-competitors` answers that. Which small set of buyers could be owned first is open. `/founder-consumer` answers that. The company package has no price. `/founder-pricing` is the skill for that price, and for whether the individual prices hold once other sellers are known.

3. **Nothing stated stops a copycat.** The subjects are landmark systems and papers named in the brief. The brief does not claim proprietary technology or know-how roughly ten times better than the next option. Community access sits with a track, and no size is given, so a network effect is not shown. Shared machines are a cost design, and the cost to serve one learner is not measured, so economies of scale are not shown. Whether the margin near 30% holds is open. `/founder-cfo` measures cost to serve and that margin. A brand, in the brief, is a name and a public site. What is known here that others do not know is not stated. The language-model sentence is the reason given for the product. A ten-year position is therefore open.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. **Engineering.** The browser machine, shared labs, and instant grading are built. Are they a breakthrough on something that matters, or an incremental way to teach these systems? The brief has the mechanism and no comparison. Open until `/founder-competitors` names the next option.

2. **Timing.** Why now is answered: language models made writing code the easy part, so systems understanding learned by doing is what remains. Whether that window is already full is open. `/founder-competitors`.

3. **Monopoly.** Where is the small market this can hold a large share of first? The brief names a wide audience, many disciplines, and a company package with no price. No share is stated. Open. `/founder-consumer` for the first buyer. `/founder-pricing` for the company price.

4. **People.** Who builds this? No team size, roles, or names are in the brief. Open.

5. **Distribution.** How do the first customers arrive, and what does each one cost? The brief states that this is not said. Open. The cost belongs to `/founder-cfo`. No channel is named here.

6. **Durability.** What still holds the position in ten years? Technology far ahead of the next option, a network effect, a measured cost advantage from shared machines, and a brand are each missing or unmeasured. Open.

7. **Secret.** What is known here that others do not know? The brief gives the language-model reason and the choice to teach systems by doing. It does not say others lack that. Open.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

None. This is a pass.

A pass would flip only if the brief showed three facts it does not show now, together: a small market and a real share of it (`/founder-consumer`), plus who already sells to that buyer (`/founder-competitors`); a stated way the first customers arrive and a cost per customer (`/founder-cfo`); and one advantage a copycat cannot match soon, among technology far ahead of the next option, a network effect with evidence, a measured cost advantage from shared labs, or a brand. The company price (`/founder-pricing`) and the unsettled margin (`/founder-cfo`) would have to be decided in that same pass, because the stated price is a wide-audience price near cost.
