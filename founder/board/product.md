# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

The moment of use is one loop. A learner reads a short lesson, then does graded work on a live machine in the browser, and can fail without taking a production system down. That loop is public at https://devsetu.io. The learner does not need a cloud account: labs are shared machines, kept efficient, with a real environment and instant grading. A project ends as a working system (an LSM tree, a B+ tree storage engine, Raft, a small SQL engine, or Cinder), so the session produces the system itself. The Whiteboard follow-on is already off.

One sentence that fits the brief: read a short lesson, then do the systems work on a live machine in the browser.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **Three products and two buyers, so nothing is the one thing.** Tracks (₹250 to ₹500 per month, community access included), projects (₹500 per minor per year, ₹750 for Cinder per year), and Paper of the Month (no price, one attempt, Whiteboard off) all sit on the same platform. Companies are a second buyer. The enterprise package for onboarding new college graduates has no price and no described session. What is already decided is tracks across engineering disciplines. What the idea says it is for is systems that hold data, traffic, and state. A customer cannot be pointed at one thing done brilliantly.

2. **The first screen is not the offer.** The working prices above are the offer. Amounts still shown in the app are not. The buyer’s first touchpoint, https://devsetu.io, is a different price from the one they are meant to pay. The experience breaks before the lab opens. Nothing in the brief describes the receipt or what happens after payment.

3. **The lab is the product, and the handoff onto it is unmeasured.** The app host is one EC2 instance (nginx, the API, Postgres, and Redis). The work runs on shared machines on the EKS cluster. Sharing is how a student gets a real machine without a cloud account, and how the price can stay near the cost of serving one learner plus roughly a 30% margin. Cost to serve one learner is not measured, and whether that margin stays is not decided. The first five minutes only work if that shared machine is actually there, is a real environment, and grades instantly. That cost is an open question for `/founder-cfo`.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- In the first five minutes on https://devsetu.io, which single path is the customer on: a track, a project, or a paper? What do they see, what do they pay, and when are they on the machine?
- What would you cut? Papers have no price and the design exercise is off. The enterprise package has no session and no price. Tracks and projects are the same loop at two lengths.
- Tracks run across engineering disciplines. The idea is systems that hold data, traffic, and state. Which of those is the product?
- Who is in the chair, and who pays? The brief names early developers, people switching into this work, and people who are aspirational, and gives no split of who pays. That is an open question for `/founder-consumer`. The company package price is an open question for `/founder-pricing`. Cost to serve one learner, and whether the margin stays near 30%, are open questions for `/founder-cfo`.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

- Cut Paper of the Month out of what a customer can buy until it has a price and a finished exercise. It is not the live-machine loop. The Whiteboard is already off.
- Put one door in front. Make the product the systems lab the projects already name, or one track that is that lab. Do not sell tracks, projects, and papers as three equal products. Do not widen to every engineering discipline until that one path is the thing done brilliantly.
- Make https://devsetu.io show the offer: ₹250 to ₹500 per track per month, ₹500 per minor project per year, ₹750 for Cinder per year. Take the other amounts off the app.
- Do not sell the enterprise package until a new college graduate’s session is this same lab and the package has a price. The price is for `/founder-pricing`.
- Measure the cost to serve one learner on the shared lab before treating “cheap enough for a wide audience” or the roughly 30% margin as settled. That measurement is `/founder-cfo`.
