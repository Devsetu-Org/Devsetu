# Offers lens
Score: 4 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong

The paid work ends in something the buyer did. A track is a short lesson, then graded exercises on a live machine in the browser, with instant grading. Community access sits with a track. A project is a longer staged build that ends in a working system: an LSM tree, a B+ tree, Raft, a small SQL engine (each a minor at ₹500 a year), or Cinder (the major, at ₹750 a year). A track is ₹250 to ₹500 a month, set by the infrastructure that track needs. The product is public at https://devsetu.io.

Two sacrifices are already removed in how it is delivered. The learner can fail without taking a production system down. Labs are shared machines, so the learner needs no cloud account of their own. That is also how the offer stays aimed at a wide audience.

The brief states why the pain is current. Language models made writing code the easy part, so the scarce skill is understanding systems that hold data, traffic, and state, learned by doing the work. Lessons and videos alone are treated as not enough.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. The market is not picked, and this lens will not fund an offer until it is. Two buyers are named. Individuals are early developers, people switching into this work, and people who are aspirational about these systems. No income, no geography, and no split of who pays. Companies are an enterprise package for onboarding new college graduates, and that package has no price. Pain is stated. Whether that pain is massive, whether those buyers have money, whether they are easy to reach, and whether the pool is growing are not in the brief. There is no stated way the first customers arrive. Who has the pain and the money is an open question for `/founder-consumer`.

2. Likelihood is the hole the brief itself marks. There is no stated guarantee, and no stated proof that learners get jobs or pass interviews. Instant grading shows that an exercise was checked. Paper of the Month is one landmark paper, short questions, one attempt, answers checked on the server, and it has no price. The Whiteboard, the follow-on design exercise, is switched off. The dream outcome in the brief is a working system. The brief gives no reason a buyer should believe that system changes what they can do. Whether buyers believe the pitch is `/founder-consumer`. What can be added as proof or a guarantee, without inventing either, is `/founder-offer`.

3. The price is a wide-audience price: about the cost of serving one learner, plus roughly a 30% margin. That cost is not measured. Whether the margin stays near 30% is not decided. Effort stays with the buyer, who still has to do the builds. Time from the start to a result they would pay for is not stated. The brief lists no bonus, no scarcity, and no urgency. This lens funds an offer a buyer feels stupid refusing: more of the outcome, sooner, with less effort, and with a guarantee. Those are not in this offer. Cost to serve, and whether any guarantee is affordable, are `/founder-cfo`. Whether these prices, or a higher one, are what buyers will pay is `/founder-pricing`. The stack that would have to carry a higher price is `/founder-offer`.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Who has this pain and the money to pay: the early developer, the switcher, the aspirational learner, or the company onboarding new college graduates? Income and who pays are open. `/founder-consumer`.
- Why would that buyer believe the work produces the result they want? The brief states no proof and no guarantee. `/founder-consumer`, then `/founder-offer`.
- How fast is the first result they would pay for: one graded exercise, a finished minor, or Cinder? Time is not in the brief. `/founder-offer`.
- What guarantee can the offer make once the cost of serving one learner is known, and once it is decided whether the margin stays near 30%? `/founder-cfo` and `/founder-offer`.
- If the price moved above that cost-plus leaning, would it still be the obvious choice? The working prices are ₹250 to ₹500 a track a month, ₹500 a minor a year, and ₹750 for Cinder a year. `/founder-pricing` and `/founder-cfo`.
- What is in the enterprise package, and what does it cost? The price is not set. `/founder-pricing`.
- Is a wide-audience price the only difference a buyer would see? The brief names no competitors. `/founder-competitors`.

## If FUND IF: the conditions

None. Pass on the offer as stated.

A pass would flip only if the open questions come back in the offer's favour together: one buyer who has the pain and can pay (`/founder-consumer`); a reason they believe the outcome, which the brief does not supply, and a guarantee the margin can carry (`/founder-offer`, `/founder-cfo`); and a price buyers accept because of the working system, with the cost to serve one learner known (`/founder-pricing`, `/founder-cfo`). If buyers show up only because the price sits near the cost of serving them, the pass stands.
